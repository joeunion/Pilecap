{% load static %} {% comment %} FRAME `sign_in` — design J2-a. The returning person, on any device. One column, one primary action, two quiet ones. The two fields are the labelled-text and password-with-Show primitives from the one token block; the alert slot under the `h1` is where every J7 refusal sentence lands, and it carries the standing second line that is printed on EVERY refusal, because a person who is locked out reads the same sentence as a person who mistyped — naming the lockout would be an oracle over which addresses exist and have been attacked. WHAT IS MARKUP AND WHAT IS NOT. The form posts to its own URL with `action=password`; `Forgot your password?` is still a same-page query link until J4 names its destination; and NOTHING here is inline script — the production CSP is `script-src 'self'` and the entrance fetches nothing it cannot serve itself. THE PASSKEY AFFORDANCE IS DRAWN IN TWO STEPS, AND BOTH CAN SAY NO. (1) The DOOR decides whether the road exists on this deployment at all (`passkey_road`, from `signin.passkey_road_open`): a relying-party id has to be a domain, an IP address is not one, and no browser will run a ceremony against `127.0.0.1` — which is what the shipped `PUBLIC_BASE_URL` is. Where the answer is no there is no button and no script tag: the page is exactly what it was. (2) The BROWSER decides whether it can do it, and that is the static file's one job here — the button ships `hidden` and `static/accounts/signin.js` unhides it where `PublicKeyCredential` exists. A button revealed by the server would be a button on a browser that cannot press it. A passkey enrolled on the identity platform's own pages does not work here — it is bound to THEIR relying-party id, measured — and the sentence for that is `passkey_elsewhere`, rendered in the alert slot above like every other refusal. {% endcomment %}
