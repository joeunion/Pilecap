# ADR config-1

**ADR config-1 · context:** under ASGI each `sync_to_async` thread owns a `DatabaseWrapper` whose connection closes only at the end of a request that thread served, so `CONN_MAX_AGE=60` pinned ~43 persistent connections against ≤4 active — flat across the ladder, unreclaimed 90s after the load stopped — on an instance with `max_connections=79` shared with Temporal and Zitadel; concurrency 1 was already at the slot wall · **decision:** Django 5.2's native psycopg pool (`DATABASES["default"]["OPTIONS"]["pool"]`), settings-only plus the `psycopg[pool]` extra, `CONN_MAX_AGE=0` derived rather than read whenever the pool is on, per-process `max_size` derived from `DJB_SERVICE_NAME` (web 10 / worker 8) (web lowered 20 → 10 and Zitadel raised 5 → 20 by DEPLOY-2, 2026-10-19, from the first live box's measurement: 5 hung the login 36 s; web's measured peak ≤ 4 active) and sized against the counted neighbour budget recorded beside the values in `deploy/env.staging.example` · **rejected:** PgBouncer transaction pooling — it forbids server-side cursors, and `.iterator()` is how exports stream chunked JSONL, how search rebuilds walk the corpus and how the workflow sweeps walk tenants, so `DISABLE_SERVER_SIDE_CURSORS` would silently turn every keyset stream into a full buffer; plus a container of surface (image, TLS on both legs, an auth story fighting the per-checkout derived database) that a ≤4-active demand cannot justify. Bare `CONN_MAX_AGE=0` with no pool — it pays a fork plus a TLS handshake per request on a *burstable* instance's CPU — measured at 71.8% of it — and it caps nothing: 32 asgiref threads under burst still open 32 slots · trigger to revisit: replicas × processes × `max_size` no longer fitting inside `max_connections` − neighbours − operator headroom — at which point PgBouncer is the next escalation and the `.iterator()` cost above is the bill to plan for · **Amended 2026-10-22 (owner, N2312, ruled 07:52 "go"):** the worker runs as `DJB_WORKER_PROCESSES` processes (2 on the production shape — `deploy/compose.yaml`'s worker `deploy.replicas`), so the app's budget is `web + processes × worker` = 26 against the shape's database's ceiling (the organs box's compose Postgres, 100); the smallest RDS class fits one worker (18) and says so by setting 1; rejected: a bigger single worker (`MAX_CONCURRENT_ACTIVITIES` past the pool — LA-WORKER-1's rejected direction) and the worker on the organs box (ruling 2's topology; `organs_cpu` 85–95 at the knee); the owner's dated ruling is the approval · **the tree is the authority:** the sizes are `config/settings/base.py::_POOL_MAX_SIZE_BY_SERVICE` (its comment cites this ADR rather than restating it), the budget is `deploy/env.staging.example`'s, and `apps/core/tests/test_connection_pool.py` drives real threads against a real Postgres and fails with the measured held count on any tree that assembles no pool.
